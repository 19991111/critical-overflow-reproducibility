\section{Statistical protocols and interpretation}\label{app:inference}
Coverage assumes uniform independent underlying words within each stream and across replicate labels. Fresh microscopic streams must also be independent of prior history. Counter-based SplitMix words and disjoint seeds provide reproducibility, not a proof of those premises. All sampling statements use this ideal word model.

The original and prospective families below are separate. Reusing a reference retains the reference event's original error cost. Cross-cell independence is unnecessary for the union bounds. The prospective statement is unconditional over the old reference and fresh data, rather than conditional on the observed reference.

\subsection{Original allocation design}\label{app:original}
The model is exactly Section~\ref*{sec:model}, with identity matchings in both directions or product crosses, $q\in\{64,128,256,512\}$, $\lambda\in\{0,1,2\}$ and $\eps=\lambda/q$. Capacity is the integer $c=q/2$, so $\kappa=1$ and $n_*=q^2$. The respective replicate counts are $128,128,256,1024$. Every paired path runs through $q^2+2q$ arrivals. There are 4608 fixed/product pairs, or 9216 individual outcomes. Every planned label was completed; none was replaced or excluded.

The 1024 seed labels are the first eight SHA256 bytes of the fixed string prefix and replicate number specified in the protocol, interpreted as a big-endian 64-bit word. Smaller scales use prefixes of this list. A counter-based SplitMix64 driver supplies two words per arrival; disjoint bit fields determine event type, direction and endpoints. All probabilities are exact power-of-two subdivisions at these parameter values. The same driver words are used within a fixed/product pair and across cells sharing a replicate label. Thus cells and checkpoints are dependent. The seed label, not a checkpoint, is the inferential unit. The confidence guarantees below are conditional on the declared ideal independent-stream reference. Reproducible pseudorandom words do not establish mathematical independence of machine streams.

Compilation and deterministic one-step tests preceded the one authorized stochastic batch. The model checks mass and capacity identities, endpoint counting, and left-tie handling. All 9216 full labelled critical configurations were subsequently re-read and independently checked against their mass, imbalance and overflow summaries. A second read-only audit reconstructed all 193536 saved checkpoint masses, overflows, product feedback counts and fixed joint-table marginals, then all 9216 outcome metrics; its largest metric roundoff difference was below $2.3\,10^{-16}$. At $\lambda=0$ all 32256 paired recorded checkpoints agree, as required. All checkpoint side histograms, fixed joint tables, driver hashes and logs are retained. The single-thread model batch took about 22.35 seconds of wall time within its fixed 360-second CPU and 420-second wall budgets. This duration is operational evidence, not an algorithm performance benchmark; no competing timed runs were active.

For phase-aware feedback there are 16 prescribed checkpoints
\[
 n_j=q^2/2+2q\left\lfloor\frac{(q/4-1)j}{15}\right\rfloor
                  +\frac{2qj}{16},\quad j=0,\ldots,15.
\]
The common phase is exactly $j/16$. The metric is the trajectory RMS of the exact conditional feedback minus $g(h_n,\Delta_n/q)$, not minus $\gb$. For overflow the metric is the maximum absolute error of $Z_c/q-S_h(\Delta/q)$ over the five fixed $h$ values $-1,-1/2,0,1/2,1$. The numerical lookup uses 257 old phase nodes with linear tail interpolation. Its uniform allowances are $0.00004861$ for feedback and $0.00002440$ for overflow, including $E+e$, interpolation and arithmetic. The interpolation addition in $d_1$ is $1/65536$, since the tail second-derivative norm is at most eight. Numerical allowances are used on both sides of each classification.

\subsection{Original failure-rate and cross-kernel intervals}
\textbf{Formal feedback/overflow and cross-kernel CDF tolerance decisions apply only at $q=512$; $q=64,128,256$ are descriptive.} Four feedback cells ($\lambda=1,2$ and both kernels) use RMS margin $0.10$. Six overflow cells (all three $\lambda$ and both kernels) use maximum-error margin $0.05$. For each cell the target is a population probability of exceeding the margin at most $0.10$. A numerically certain failure exceeds the margin even after subtracting its allowance; a possible failure exceeds it after adding the allowance. Exact binomial tail inversion on a conservative $0.001$ grid bounds the failure probability below using certain failures and above using possible failures. Each side uses error probability $1/800$, giving simultaneous coverage at least $0.975$ over the ten cells by union bound. Entirely interior intervals support the target, lower bounds above it detect mismatch, and all other cases are inconclusive.

Cross-kernel comparison uses endpoint CDFs of $\Delta_{q^2}/q$ and $Z_c(q^2)/q$ at $\lambda=1,2$. It does not use raw common-random-number path distances: the driver coupling is not the maximal-load-pair rank coupling of Section~\ref*{sec:universality}. The frozen practical CDF-distance margin is $0.20$. DKW--Massart bounds \cite{massart} of width $0.057$ for each of the eight empirical CDFs yield simultaneous pair bands $0.114$, with family error at most $0.025$ because
\[
 16\exp[-2\cdot1024\cdot(0.057)^2]<0.025.
\]
The scalar inequality was checked with exact rational Taylor bounds. Independence between the two kernels is unnecessary for the union bound. Observed KS distances, in order $\lambda=1$ imbalance/overflow and $\lambda=2$ imbalance/overflow, are $9/1024$, $17/1024$, $13/1024$ and $1/64$. The true CDF-distance upper bounds are respectively $0.122790$, $0.130602$, $0.126696$ and $0.129625$ after upward rounding. All are below $0.20$, supporting this specified finite-size equivalence criterion. The lower bounds are zero; no positive separation is established. Adding the primary and CDF family errors bounds the total simultaneous error by $0.05$ under the reference model.

The source freeze is dated 17 October 2026, 23:42:34 UTC. The input freeze precedes the first trajectory, and the data freeze precedes inference.

For label $i$, form the string
\begin{center}\small\texttt{paper\_micro\_prospective\_v1|replicate=\%05d}\end{center}
with $i$ formatted as five digits. Interpret its first eight SHA256 bytes as a big-endian 64-bit seed. All 4096 values are unique and disjoint from the original and reference seed lists. \subsection{Prospective intervals and coverage allocation}
The fixed-time nonnegative-product argument is a simple instance of betting-based mean inference \cite{betting}. For means, clip $V_i=\min(Z_i/q,3)$ only for inference and retain all unclipped observations. Lower candidates $m\in[0,1]$ use $\prod_i[1+V_i-m]$; upper candidates $m\in[1/2,3]$ use $\prod_i[1+(2/5)(m-V_i)]$. Reject only at capital at least 1000, and invert on the fixed $10^{-5}$ grid outwards using exact integer products. Each endpoint costs at most $0.001$ by the fixed-time nonnegative-product bound; no normal approximation is used.

Changing one arrival changes final labelled-load $\ell_1$ distance by at most two and overflow by at most one. At $q^2$ arrivals, bounded differences thus gives $\Prob(X-\E X>t)\le e^{-2t^2}$ for $X=Z/q$. The fixed coarse bound $U=\overline X+0.030$ costs at most $e^{-2R(0.030)^2}<0.001$ per cell. On $\E X\le U<3$, add the upward-evaluated clipping-bias bound
\[
 \E(X-3)_+\le\frac{1}{4(3-U)}e^{-2(3-U)^2}.
\]
All four mean-width gates $\le0.04$ passed. No sample exceeded three, but the population tail allowance was still included.

The existing reference's four fixed mean endpoints each cost at most $0.001$. Its clipped mean intervals are $[0.69875,0.70333]$ at $\lambda=1$ and $[0.67391,0.67781]$ at $\lambda=2$. The audited total approximation and truncation allowances $0.0006$ and $0.0010$ give true SDE mean intervals $[0.69815,0.70393]$ and $[0.67291,0.67881]$. Both satisfy the unchanged width gate $0.010$. Subtraction of these intervals from the fresh unconditional micro intervals gives Table~\ref*{tab:prospective}. Eight fresh betting endpoints, four fresh coarse bounds and four existing reference endpoints cost at most $0.016$.

Fresh micro CDFs use DKW width $a=0.032$ with $R=4096$; the two existing reference CDF events use $b=0.012$ with $N_{\rm ref}=32768$. The audited reference-to-SDE coupling supplies horizontal radius $r_1=0.010$, $r_2=0.020$ and vertical allowance $\tau=0.005$. With $A$ the micro empirical law, $B$ the computed reference empirical law, $B_\pm=B\pm r_\lambda$, $d(A,B)=\sup_x(F_A-F_B)$ and $\delta=a+b+\tau=0.049$, the distance bounds are
\begin{align*}
 L&=\max\{0,\max[d(A,B_-),d(B_+,A)]-\delta\},\\
 U&=\min\{1,\max[d(A,B_+),d(B_-,A)]+\delta\}.
\end{align*}
Every jump, including ties, is included; stored long-double hexadecimal values are parsed as exact rationals. The unchanged reference CDF-envelope width gate $0.10$ passed for both $\lambda$ values. The simultaneous failure allocation for this family's eight comparisons is
\begin{align*}
 \alpha_{\rm mean}&\le0.016,\\
 \alpha_{\rm CDF}&\le8e^{-2\cdot4096(0.032)^2}
                +4e^{-2\cdot32768(0.012)^2},\\
 \alpha_{\rm joint}&<0.018138367441<0.05.
\end{align*}
The scalar exponential bounds were certified before sampling with rational Taylor bounds. This is at least $95\%$ simultaneous coverage for these eight new comparisons under the contracts below. It is a separate family from the original finite-batch claims, not a combined confidence statement for every result in the manuscript. Between-cell independence is not needed for the union bounds.
