## Fixed-recipe coverage is not automatically selected-recipe coverage

Let X denote the old data, a a stake/clipping/inference recipe, and C_a(X) its interval. The fixed-rule assertion

for every deterministic a: P(theta in C_a(X)) >=1-alpha

does not imply

P(theta in C_A(X)(X)) >=1-alpha

when A is chosen after inspecting X. Conditioning on A(X)=a generally changes the distribution of the same observations. For a fixed admissible stake, independence makes a product of nonnegative factors have expectation at most one under the appropriate null. If that stake is selected from the completed data, it is a random function of the factors; the original expectation factorization no longer proves the same bound. Maximizing or choosing among valid capitals needs its own mixture, uniform bound, multiplicity control, predictable construction, or independent data. The original bundle has no such argument covering its actual choice of clipping B, stakes and inference recipe.

Predictable betting choices may use the past before the next observation, which is different from inspecting the completed sample and rerunning all factors with a selected constant. This distinction is part of the conditions in [Waudby-Smith and Ramdas, Estimating means of bounded random variables by betting](https://arxiv.org/html/2010.09686v7). We do not claim their general results automatically establish the present selected recipe.

The nominal16-tail accounting controls the specified fixed recipe under its assumptions. It does not include the unrecorded recipe-selection step. The previous whole-workflow joint95% statement is therefore withdrawn. Ideal iid uniform input words across replicates and within every underlying stream, exact BigInt arithmetic, directed Decimal checks and independent new reference draws do not repair missing selection control for the old micro mean intervals.
