Conclusion. Under the arithmetic contract below, each computed Box--Muller coordinate differs from the exact midpoint-uniform coordinate by less than 1e-12. In particular the frozen 1e-9 allowance is valid. State rounding plus readout rounding divided by .5001 is less than 4e-12, hence the single frozen 1e-6 allowance covers both. 0. Arithmetic contract and constants
All basic +,-,*,/ operations are binary round-to-nearest with p>=64 significant bits; write u=2^-64<1e-19. For a normal nonzero result fl(z)=z(1+delta), |delta|<=u. Exact zero is preserved. No operation considered here overflows or underflows. frexp and ldexp are exact radix-two operations. Hexadecimal input of the stored binary64 table values into long double is exact. The compiled code disables fast-math and contraction. The executable's type checks and saved environment support this contract; the conclusion remains conditional on the actual arithmetic obeying it.

Use gamma_k=ku/(1-ku). Throughout k<=1000, so gamma_k<1.001ku. The elementary product and summation bounds follow by expanding products of (1+delta), or by the standard induction giving gamma_k. No library log/sin/cos/sqrt accuracy is assumed for sampling.

The pre-sampling exporter uses exact Fraction series intervals [pilo,pihi] and [lnlo,lnhi], and checks PI in (pilo-4u,pihi+4u), LN2 in (lnlo-u,lnhi+u). Each series-interval width is <.01u, directly from its displayed geometric/alternating remainder (80 Machin terms, 100 atanh terms). Thus the actual checks imply
 |PI-pi|<4.01u, |LN2-ln(2)|<1.01u.
The relative error of LN2 is <2u, because ln(2)>2/3. We include the rational-series enclosure width; it is not valid to silently drop it or to assert both relative constant errors are <=u.

The uniforms are exact dyadic midpoints U=(j+1/2)2^-52, so 2^-53<=U<=1-2^-53. The exact normal radius is below 9 since -2log(U)<=106log(2)<81. All series and Newton intermediates are normal finite numbers; even the smallest radius is of order 1e-8, far above underflow. Potential cancellation to zero in a state addition is harmless under an absolute-error bound.

1. ownlog: relative error <=1000u+T
For x in [1/2,2], t=(x-1)/(x+1). Sterbenz makes the numerator subtraction exact; the denominator and division give relative error at most gamma_2<3u. The exact |t|<=1/3. The program computes the 24 terms 2 sum_{j=0}^{23} t^(2j+1)/(2j+1), which all have the same sign.

Each reciprocal coefficient has relative error <=u. The computed t^2 has relative error at most gamma_7. The j-th iterated power therefore has a product-error index at most 3+8j<=187; its coefficient multiplication adds at most 2. Summing 24 same-sign terms adds at most 23. A conservative 250u relative bound covers the entire polynomial computation. Multiplication by 2 is exact. At t=0 both exact and computed logarithms are zero.

The exact omitted tail divided by |log(x)| is at most
 T=3^-48/[49(1-1/9)]<1e-24,
using |log(x)|>=2|t|. For example 3^12=531441 makes this last numerical inequality elementary. Hence the direct branch has relative error <=250u+T.

For U<1/2, frexp reduction writes U=m 2^e with 1<=m<2 and -53<=e<=-2, exactly. The preceding bound applies to log(m). The computed e*LN2 has relative error <4u. Since |log U|>=log 2, log m<=|log U| and |e|log 2<=2|log U|, the final possibly subtractive addition causes no large condition number: its error relative to |log U| is at most
 250u+T+8u+3u <1000u+T.
This also explains why the direct branch near U=1 is necessary: an absolute log error alone would not control its tiny radius.

2. ownsqrt: relative error <=2000u+3e-64
The code normalizes a positive input exactly to m in [1/2,2), using an even exponent, and performs seven Newton steps s <- (s+m/s)/2 from s0=1.

For exact Newton arithmetic, the error coordinate r=(s-sqrt(m))/(s+sqrt(m)) is squared at each step. Initially |r0|<1/5, so after seven steps |r7|<5^-128<1e-64. Thus the relative truncation error is <3e-64.

To bound rounding without needing a sharp stability argument, all exact iterates lie in [sqrt(1/2),1.5]. On the wider interval [.7,1.6], the derivative of the exact Newton map has absolute value <2. One rounded step has absolute local error <10u: m/s<3, the intermediate sum is <4.6, and division by 2 is exact. Induction therefore bounds the computed-vs-exact iterate error after j steps by 10u(2^j-1). At j=7 this is 1270u, and division by sqrt(m)>=sqrt(1/2) makes it <2000u. The same induction stays strictly inside [.7,1.6], so there is no circular range assumption. Final ldexp scaling is exact.

Applying sqrt to the computed -2*ownlog(U), the input's relative error is at most 1000u+T; multiplication by -2 is exact. For |delta|<1/2, |sqrt(1+delta)-1|<=|delta|. Combining the input and Newton bounds, a deliberately loose relative radius error is <10000u+1e-23. As the exact radius is <9, its absolute error is <1e-14.

3. sine/cosine and coordinate multiplication
The quadrant calculation uses exact dyadic 4V and exact subtraction of its integer part. Multiplying the remainder by PI/2 gives absolute angle error <5u compared with the exact reduced angle, whose range is [0,pi/2]. Indeed the constant contributes <2.005u and the rounded multiplication contributes <1.6u. The computed angle is <1.6.

The factorial loop up to 34 and reciprocal conversion give every inverse-factorial coefficient relative error <40u. Each cosine/sine Horner polynomial has 16 multiply-subtract stages. The generic signed-polynomial absolute roundoff bound is gamma_32 times its absolute-coefficient sum, plus the coefficient errors. That sum is bounded by exp(1.6)<5. The one rounded square of the angle may equivalently be represented by a relative perturbation <u of the angle; the final sine multiplication adds one operation. Combining these contributions, the angle-constant error, and the Lipschitz constant 1 of exact sin/cos, a safe absolute error is
 1000u + 2e-24
for each reduced sine or cosine. Exact sign changes and quadrant swaps add no error.

For completeness the Taylor remainders are smaller than 2e-24: both are bounded by 1.6^34/34! or 1.6^35/35!. The elementary inequalities 34!>=10!*11^10*21^14>1e34 and 2^34<2e10 suffice. Thus no opaque transcendental call or numerical grid test enters the proof.

Multiplying radius and trig value, with one rounded multiplication, gives coordinate error less than
 1e-14 + 10*(1000u+2e-24) + 10u <1e-12.
This is already a thousand times smaller than the frozen uniform 1e-9 coordinate allowance. The deterministic boundary tests are supplementary only.

4. Midpoint Gaussian coupling and accumulated driver error
Let Delta=2^-52 and couple a continuous uniform to the midpoint of its cell. For R(v)=sqrt(-2log v), the first cell's integrated squared radius error is bounded by
 4Delta[1+log 2+2log(1/Delta)].
For the interior cells, Cauchy--Schwarz gives at most (Delta^2/2) times the integral of 1/[v^2(-2log v)] over [Delta,1-Delta]. Splitting at 1/2 bounds this contribution by 2Delta. The final cell contributes at most 8Delta^2/(1-Delta).
The angle differs by at most pi Delta. Convexity of -log gives E R(midpoint)^2<=2. The inequality (a+b)^2<=2a^2+2b^2 now yields, for each coordinate,
 E|Z-Zmid|^2 <=100Delta(1+log(1/Delta))<1e-12.
The last inequality is also checked conservatively as 3800/2^52<1e-12 in the exporter.

Hence each increment has L2 normal error <=1e-6+1e-9 under the frozen allowances. N=16384 increments are scaled by 1/sqrt(N)=1/128. Approximate Euler-map contraction plus Minkowski gives total L2 driver error <=128*(1e-6+1e-9)<.00013. This step does not assume cancellation or independence of the coordinate errors.

To identify the ideal target increments as independent Gaussian increments, the underlying word model must be uniform and independent both within each stream and across replicate streams. Merely assuming independent seeds/replicates does not establish within-stream IID. The actual deterministic word(seed,j) implementation does not prove this premise.

5. State and output rounding, jointly
Here treat the computed normal coordinates as exact inputs; their error was already charged above. Exact dyadic h and sqrt(h) make their scaling exact. Stored binary64 lookup entries are exactly representable in long double.
Inside the feedback table, multiplication of |state| by 512 and extraction of the fractional part are exact. Each table difference has magnitude <=2/512 and each table value has magnitude <=1. One subtraction, multiplication and addition therefore give feedback-evaluation error <10u. Constant extension and odd reflection are exact.

For |state|<1155, the sum h*lambda*g+sqrt(h)*normal has magnitude <.071. Its addition rounding, the feedback-evaluation error times h*lambda, and the final state addition together give absolute per-step error <2000u. The same-state exact approximate-drift Euler map is 1-Lipschitz, so N steps accumulate at most N*2000u<3.3e-12. The exact deterministic range is <=9sqrt(N)+2=1154, leaving a unit margin, so the rounded range assumption closes by induction.

The final readout interpolates values at most 16 for |state|<32; its three arithmetic operations have absolute error <100u. Outside this interval it returns |state|/2 exactly. Therefore
 state-roundoff + readout-roundoff/.5001 <4e-12<1e-6.
This joint bound is what the mean-error formula needs. It is not justified by assigning 1e-6 separately to both terms; the explicit smaller bounds above justify the single shared allowance.

6. Sealable scope
The frozen 1e-9 per-coordinate arithmetic and 1e-6 joint state/output arithmetic allowances are supported under the stated numerical contract, without running anything new. Together with the audited midpoint coupling, monotone Euler bound, global feedback/readout table errors and Gaussian clipping tail, the stored .0006 and .0010 mean approximation budgets and .010/.020 horizontal CDF brackets with .005 vertical coupling allowance are mathematically supported in the ideal IID-word model.
This supplement is an analytic audit, not a machine-checked floating-point verification or a proof of PRNG independence. A reproducible environment/type/selftest check is evidence for the contract, not a replacement for it.
