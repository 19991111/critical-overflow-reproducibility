        CRITICAL OVERFLOW IN WEAKLY COUPLED TWO-CHOICE
                          ALLOCATION
                                                   SILAS LU∗

          Abstract. We determine which candidate information a reduced model must retain to predict
5    critical overflow in a fixed two-choice allocation rule. Two communities of q buckets have uniform
     bipartite internal sampling and cross sampling of probability ε. Each cross direction uses a prede-
     termined doubly stochastic kernel; ties favor the left endpoint. For empty starts, εq → λ < ∞,
     and integer capacity c with 2c/q → κ > 0, we prove a common joint limit for imbalance and the
     overflow curve on the q 2 arrival scale. A coupling compares the actual fixed-kernel and product
10   processes. An exact midpoint evolution proves attraction to a unique two-side lattice profile. The
     imbalance converges to a scalar diffusion whose drift averages the fast common phase. Overflow
     retains the observation phase and the internal profile. Community excess alone therefore misses a
     positive order-q contribution to expected overflow. At nominal capacity, expected overflow is order q,
     while its fraction tends to zero. Joint first Wasserstein convergence justifies both distributional and
15   mean conclusions. Certified profile evaluation and preserved samples assess the separate prediction
     steps at specified finite-size tolerances. No effective uniform error bound for the joint critical limit
     is asserted.

          Key words. randomized allocation, two choices, graphical load balancing, overflow, universality,
     diffusion approximation

          1. Introduction. Two-choice allocation assigns each item to the less loaded of
     two candidates [2]. We study the total number of items assigned beyond bucket
     capacity. At total nominal capacity, overflow equals half the total absolute deviation
     from capacity. Its prediction requires information beyond the largest load.
25        We ask which information a reduced model must retain for two weakly coupled
     communities. Each community has q buckets with uniform internal bipartite can-
     didates. A fraction ε of candidates connect the communities through any predeter-
     mined doubly stochastic kernel. Both fixed matchings and product sampling have the
     required uniform endpoint marginals. Ties favor the left endpoint, as in the internal
30   two-group Always-Go-Left rule of Vöcking [13].
          With empty starts and integer capacity c  q, critical fill occurs at n∗ = 2qc 
     q 2 arrivals. Without cross sampling, community choices produce order-q imbalance
     fluctuations. Cross arrivals can contribute order εq to the cumulative normalized drift.
     These scales motivate weak coupling εq → λ < ∞.
35        Every admissible fixed cross kernel has the same first-order critical law. Internal
     load variation remains in that law, even at known community imbalance. Expected
     overflow is order q, while its fraction of arrivals tends to zero. Changing the fixed
     cross kernel preserves its leading coeﬀicient.

        1.2. Relation to previous work. Existing rules and distribution equations.
   Vöcking [13] introduced Always-Go-Left. Berenbrink, Czumaj, Steger, and Vöck-
   ing [4] give sharp heavy-load deviation bounds for greedy and asymmetric allocation.
65 Mitzenmacher [6] uses differential equations to describe full load distributions. We
   use this established distribution-level viewpoint. The additional requirements here
   are long-time attraction of a two-type integer-lattice profile and its coupling to a slow
   community imbalance.
        Correlated choices and process comparison. Mitzenmacher [7, 8] shows asymptotic
70 equivalence between double hashing and fully random balanced allocation, using fluid
   limits and a coupling through a biased intermediate process. The stated distribution
   results concern a constant number of choices and T N arrivals into N bins for fixed
   T . Our comparison treats arbitrary fixed doubly stochastic cross kernels, allows only
   order-q −1 cross probability, and covers T q 2 arrivals. It bounds the distance between

        Graphs and queues. Peres, Talwar, and Wieder [10] study graphical allocation
   and limited choice. Bansal and Feldheim [3] give a strategy based on graph decompo-
   sitions. Our rule compares only the two endpoint loads. Queueing universality and
80 diffusion limits [9, 5, 1] include service and use different scales. Here each item has
   two candidates and loads only increase.
        Traveling profiles. Stolyar [11, 12] develops traveling-wave and stationary-limit
   methods. Contraction and compactness are precedents. The existence result in [11,
   Theorem 3] permits general jumps. Its uniqueness and attraction result [11, Theorem
85 4] requires a positive jump density bounded away from zero on compact subsets of
   R+ . The arithmetic-jump caveat following that theorem prevents its direct use for
   the present lattice dynamics. We prove the two-side periodic-family result directly,
   then obtain finite-system tracking without a stationary-limit interchange. The later
   phase averaging acts on the slow drift, while the critical overflow retains its endpoint
90 phase.
